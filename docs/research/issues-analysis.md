## What this implies for an open reimplementation

1. **Thermals are the hardest honest problem.** 160 titles and 272 reports mention temperature or a sensor.
   The original answers with provider-specific escape hatches (AMD's Monitoring SDK plus elevation on
   Windows; `powercap` and SMBIOS permissions on Linux). A clone must treat *unavailable* as a
   first-class answer and say exactly which grant is missing — that is already the original's stated
   design rule, and it is the right one.
2. **Freezes and gaps are systematic, not exotic.** 134 reports mention a crash, freeze or hang and
   174 mention a gap, jump or wrong reading, in titles as well as bodies. The original's own support
   answer is an environment variable that writes a sampling log. Per-provider isolation, timeouts and
   a watchdog are therefore table stakes, not polish.
3. **Licensing and updates are a support burden.** 133 titles, and 564 reports anywhere, touch the
   checkout, activation or update path. Removing the paywall and the activation server removes the
   whole class; keeping updates manual and signed removes most of the rest.
4. **Platform parity leaks.** Windows dominates the tracker; macOS and Linux report the sensor and
   packaging gaps. A provider model with a capability matrix makes the gaps visible instead of
   surprising.
5. **Nobody is asking for a prettier skin.** The requests that repeat are accuracy, missing sensors,
   export, columns, keyboard and accessibility. The aesthetics are already good enough to be the
   benchmark; the substance is where a competitor wins.

## Caveats

- Title matching is crude. `missing` counts a missing sensor and a missing column alike.
- Closed does not mean fixed: maintainers close reports after moving them to an internal tracker.
- The tracker is self-selected. Silent users are not in these numbers.
- Counts move. Re-run the script against a fresh JSON to refresh.
